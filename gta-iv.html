Niko Bellic — The Best GTA Protagonist

I'm going to say it: Niko Bellic is the best protagonist in GTA history. He's complex, flawed, and genuinely sympathetic. He's done terrible things in the war, he's trying to leave that behind, but the past keeps dragging him back. His relationship with his cousin Roman is the heart of the game. Roman is an idiot — he makes bad decisions, he gambles away their money, he gets them into trouble. But he loves Niko, and Niko loves him. The voice acting by Michael Hollick is incredible — you can hear the pain and exhaustion in his voice. The mobile port preserves all the cutscenes and dialogue, and they hold up beautifully. That final choice — "Deal" or "Revenge" — is still agonizing after all these years.

Liberty City — Alive and Breathing

GTA 4's Liberty City is a masterpiece of world design. It's not glamorous like Vice City or varied like San Andreas. It's a grimy, rain-soaked, overcrowded urban jungle. And it's alive. Pedestrians have conversations with each other. Cabbies yell at you when you steal their cars. Radio stations react to your actions. The city feels like it exists independently of you. The mobile port scales back the graphics, but the atmosphere remains intact. The draw distance is shorter, the textures are lower resolution, but the soul of Liberty City is there. Driving through Broker at night while listening to the jazz station is a vibe. The Euphoria physics make every car crash feel dramatic — watch cars crumple realistically and pedestrians stumble drunkenly out of bars.

Driving and Combat — Weighty and Brutal

GTA 4's driving physics are divisive. Cars handle like boats — they have weight, inertia, and momentum. You can't just accelerate around corners and expect to grip. You need to brake, slide, and modulate the throttle. It's frustrating at first, but once it clicks, it's incredibly satisfying. The mobile port simplifies the handling slightly, but the core feel is there. The combat is brutal — the Euphoria physics mean enemies stumble when shot, grab onto ledges for support, and cry out when wounded. The cover system is essential — you can snap to walls, blind-fire, and pop out for headshots. The mobile controls take some getting used to, but the auto-aim helps. A controller is highly recommended.
