PlayStation 5 Pro Review: Is the Upgrade Worth It for Gamers in 2026?

Published: April 29, 2026 by Oli Sa 10 min read
"The PlayStation 5 Pro is an undeniable technical marvel—but at a staggering $900 and with a disc drive sold separately, it's the console that only the most dedicated enthusiasts can truly afford to love."

The PlayStation 5 Pro has finally hit its stride. When it launched in late 2024, the general feeling was one of justified skepticism. With a high price tag and a tiny list of games that actually showed a noticeable leap forward, many wondered if Sony had misjudged the room . Fast forward to April 2026, and the narrative has completely flipped. Between major software updates, a game library finally capable of harnessing this hardware, and the looming weight of upcoming releases, the PS5 Pro has become the definitive console that enthusiasts desire. There's just one massive problem: your wallet .

The PSSR Revolution: Finally, No More Compromises

The true turning point arrived in March 2026 with the rollout of PSSR 2 (PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution) . This AI-powered upscaling technology has reached a level of maturity that resolves the biggest dilemma of this generation: the painful choice between "Quality Mode" and "Performance Mode."

PSSR 2 has made the once-utopian dream a reality: playing AAA titles at maximum fidelity with stable 60FPS, without the blur, visual artifacts, or loss of detail caused by aggressive dynamic resolutions . Titles like Final Fantasy VII Rebirth and Cyberpunk 2077 have been completely transformed on this machine, offering a level of sharpness and visual experience that, for the first time in console gaming, rivals mid-to-high-range PCs .

A technical powerhouse that's finally been unlocked by PSSR 2, but its sky-high price makes it a tough sell for the average gamer.

Hardware Deep Dive: What's Actually Inside?

The PS5 Pro is built around a 16.7 teraflop GPU, which is a massive 62% increase in raw graphical compute over the standard PS5 . It also comes with 2GB of extra DDR5 memory (18GB total) and double the storage with a 2TB SSD . However, it still uses the same AMD Zen 2 CPU cores, meaning this is very much an evolution, not a revolution .

Ray Tracing and Visual Fidelity

The PS5 Pro features approximately three times the ray-tracing performance power of the standard PS5 . When developers actually utilize it, the results are stunning. Assassin's Creed Shadows now runs with ray-traced global illumination at 60fps, completely uncompromised, while the standard PS5 chugs along at a shaky 30fps .

At the recent Global Game Developers Conference, a technical demo of F1 25 showcased full path-traced rendering running on the PS5 Pro—marking the first time in console history that this highest standard of real-time graphical rendering has been achieved on a consumer device . The natural shadow transitions, precise mirror reflections, and soft ambient lighting were unprecedented for a console .

The Hidden Cost of the Pro: Your Display Matters

To truly see the benefits of the PS5 Pro, you need a display that can keep up. If you own a 65-inch or 55-inch TV and sit 10 feet away or more, the improved visual fidelity simply isn't tangible enough at that distance . However, if you have a 4K display with HDMI 2.1, VRR (Variable Refresh Rate), and 120Hz support, the difference is night and day.

Many Pro Enhanced games, like Spider-Man 2 and The Last of Us Part I, support unlocked framerates, allowing them to hit up to 120fps . The additional 2GB of memory and faster GPU make 4K gaming at 60fps simultaneously possible without the need for compromises .

The Elephant in the Room: The PS6 Looms

The PS5 Pro in 2026 is a technological statement piece. It's proof that Sony's vision for combining AI and resolution was correct. But now that the console has finally reached its peak utility and performance, the entry barrier has become nearly insurmountable. With Sony's successive price hikes pushing the machine into luxury territory—€900 without a disc drive—you're entering the realm of building a competent gaming PC .

More critically, we're at the point where reports and supplier rumors about the PlayStation 6 are already circulating . Recommending an investment of nearly a thousand dollars in a transitional machine when the next generation will bring far larger architectural leaps at a similar price point is financially irresponsible for most consumers .

Who Should Actually Buy This?

The PS5 Pro is currently the best console for a very specific type of gamer: the die-hard enthusiast with a high-end 4K/120Hz display who simply cannot wait for GTA VI to drop on November 26, 2026 . If you are a framerate advocate with no budget constraints and want to play the best of the current generation with maximum quality, there has never been a better time to jump .

But for the average gamer? The PS5 Pro is nothing more than an excellent technological showcase to admire from a distance . The irony of this generation is clear: the console has finally achieved all the potential we demanded of it, but with a $900 price tag so close to the next generation, it has become an unreasonable luxury that the vast majority will simply ignore while saving their wallets for the PlayStation 6 .

Final Verdict: A Masterpiece You Can't Afford to Love

If you're a new buyer without a PS5, you own a top-tier 4K/120Hz display, and you don't mind buying a disc drive separately, the PS5 Pro is worth the long-term investment . It delivers a premium gaming experience that no other console can match.
