Why GTA 3 Still Matters Today

Look, the graphics are dated. The controls are clunky compared to modern games. Claude, the protagonist, doesn't speak a single word. But here's the thing, GTA 3's design is still brilliant. The mission structure is open-ended, you can choose which jobs to take, when to do them, and how to approach each objective. The three-island structure keeps you engaged, the first island is the tutorial, the second island ramps up the difficulty, and the third island is chaos mode. The side activities, vigilante missions, ambulance missions, taxi fares, are genuinely fun diversions. And the sense of freedom is still intoxicating. You can ignore the story entirely and just cause mayhem for hours. The mobile version runs well on modern devices and includes touch-optimized controls.

The Radio Stations: Still the Best in the Series

I'm going to make a bold claim: GTA 3 has the best radio stations of any GTA game. Chatterbox FM, hosted by the late, great Ricky Gervais (yes, that Ricky Gervais, this was before The Office), is absolute gold. It's a parody talk show with callers complaining about everything from immigrants to video games. The satire is sharp, the writing is hilarious, and it still holds up. Double Clef FM is classical music for when you want to mow down pedestrians with some culture. Head Radio is soft rock that shouldn't work but does. Rise FM is drum and bass that makes night driving feel epic. And MSX FM is jungle music for when you're in a rampage mood. The sound design overall is excellent, the car engines, the police sirens, the pedestrian screams, it all comes together to create Liberty City's atmosphere.

The Story: Silent But Effective

Claude is a silent protagonist, but the story works anyway. You start as a nobody, betrayed by your girlfriend Catalina (who would later appear in San Andreas), and left for dead. You work your way up through the criminal underworld, taking jobs for the mafia, the Yakuza, and a corrupt businessman. The characters are memorable, Asuka, Maria, Salvatore, and 8-Ball (the bomb expert). The ending is satisfying. The cutscenes are basic by today's standards, but the voice acting is solid. For a game released in 2001, the storytelling is surprisingly effective. The mobile port preserves all the cutscenes and dialogue.

The Vehicles: Steal Everything That Moves

The vehicle variety in GTA 3 is impressive for its time. Cars, trucks, vans, SUVs, taxis, police cars, ambulances, fire trucks, and even a tank. Each vehicle handles differently, sports cars are fast but fragile, trucks are slow but durable, and the Banshee is the best all-around car. You can modify some vehicles (like the Kuruma) by driving it into a Pay 'n' Spray. The car physics are arcadey but satisfying, you can drift, jump, and even fly a bit off ramps. The mobile touch controls take some getting used to, but the game includes auto-acceleration and tilt steering options. If you have a controller, use it, it's much better.

Liberty City: Three Islands of Chaos

Liberty City is GTA 3's secret weapon. The game is split into three islands, Portland (industrial, Italian mafia territory), Staunton Island (urban, Yakuza territory), and Shoreside Vale (suburban, Colombian cartel territory). Each island has its own look, feel, and enemy factions. The world is smaller than modern open-world games, but it's dense. Every street is memorable. The shortcuts are rewarding. The atmosphere is perfect, grimy, dangerous, and alive. The lighting and weather effects (night, rain, fog) add atmosphere. Even 20+ years later, exploring Liberty City is a joy.

Is This Still Worth Playing in 2026?

Absolutely. GTA 3 is a museum piece, it's a look at the game that changed everything. The controls are dated, the graphics are old, and the story is simple. But the freedom, the chaos, and the atmosphere are still intoxicating. If you're a younger gamer who started with GTA 5, you might find GTA 3 lacking. But if you want to see where it all began, where the three-island structure, the radio stations, the side missions, and the open-world sandbox were born, it's essential. The mobile port runs well, the touch controls are serviceable, and the OBB ensures you get the full experience. Just don't forget to save your game. There's no autosave in this one, old-school style.
