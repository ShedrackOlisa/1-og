Why FTS 25 Is Great for Casual Play

I'm going to be honest here. First Touch Soccer isn't trying to compete with EA FC Mobile or eFootball on simulation depth. It's a completely different product. The matches are shorter, the gameplay is faster, and the learning curve is much gentler. You can pick it up and be scoring goals within minutes. But that's exactly why I love it. When I'm on the bus or waiting for an appointment, I don't want to spend five minutes setting up tactics. I want to play. FTS 25 delivers that. The AI is decent without being frustrating, you'll concede goals, but you'll also score plenty. The difficulty settings actually make a meaningful difference, which isn't always the case in mobile games.

What's Updated in the 2025 Mod

The modding community has done solid work here. All the major leagues have updated kits for the 2024/25 season, Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga, Ligue 1. The transfers are current, so you'll find all the summer moves reflected. Player faces for top stars have been improved where possible. The gameplay is smoother than previous versions, the modders have tweaked the animations and responsiveness. The menus have a cleaner look. And the best part? It runs on low-end devices. I've tested this on a 2019 budget phone, and it holds a steady 60fps. That's something you can't say about most modern football games.

Game Modes: Perfect for Short Sessions

FTS 25 offers a few different ways to play. Exhibition mode is for quick matches, pick your teams and go. League mode lets you play through a full season with your chosen club. Cup mode is straightforward knockout action. There's also a "Career Lite" mode, it's not as deep as FIFA's career mode, but you can manage transfers, lineups, and multiple seasons. For someone like me who wants to play, not micromanage, it's perfect. The matches themselves take about 5-8 minutes, which is ideal for mobile gaming. You can finish a game during a coffee break. And because there's no energy system, you can play as many matches as you want.

Quick Tips From My Experience:
• Use the sprint button sparingly, your players will tire out by the 70th minute if you hold it constantly.
• The through ball is very effective in this game. Learn to time it right.
• If you're losing, switch to an attacking formation. It works more often than not.
• The game has a "quick substitution" feature during pauses, use it to keep your team fresh.
• If the game lags, go to Settings > Graphics and disable "High Quality Textures". Still looks good, runs better.

Controls and Gameplay

The control scheme is one of FTS's strongest features. There's a dedicated shoot button, a pass button, and a sprint button. That's it for basic controls. For advanced moves, you can swipe on the shoot button for lob shots, or double-tap the pass button for a through ball. The responsiveness is excellent, I've never felt like the game ignored my inputs. The AI is decent without being cheap. Defenders will track runs, but they're not superhuman. Goalkeepers make reasonable saves, some spectacular, some frustrating. The pace is faster than simulation games, which makes every match feel exciting. You'll score more goals, concede more goals, and have more fun.

Visuals and Performance

Let's be real, this isn't a next-gen graphics showcase. The player models are stylized, the stadiums are simplified, and the animations are basic. But that's by design. The entire game is under 1GB, which means it runs on basically any Android phone from the last 7-8 years. On my test device (a 2019 mid-ranger), it runs at a locked 60fps with no frame drops. The menus are snappy, and loading times are minimal. For what this game is trying to be, a quick, accessible arcade football experience, the visual sacrifices are completely justified. The mod has improved textures compared to the original, and the updated kits look crisp. You won't mistake it for a console game, but you also won't care once you're in the action.
