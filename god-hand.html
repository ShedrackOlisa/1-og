The Combat — Like Jazz Improvisation With Punches

The combat system in God Hand is unlike anything else. You can chain together 100+ custom combos, assigning different punches, kicks, and wrestling moves to four attack buttons. Want a string of jabs followed by a roundhouse kick? Go for it. Prefer a suplex into a gut punch? You can do that. The game encourages experimentation — you'll find new moves hidden throughout the levels, each adding to your arsenal. The dodge system is unique too — instead of a roll, you can weave left or right with a flick of the stick. Time it perfectly, and you'll enter "God Hand" mode, where your attacks become faster, stronger, and flashier. It's a risk-reward system that rewards aggressive play.

Customization — Build Your Own Fighter

This is where God Hand really shines. You can assign any unlocked move to any of the four attack buttons. You want to start a combo with a powerful uppercut and end with a spinning kick? You can do that. Want to map a grab move to your basic attack button? Weird, but you can. The game has over 100 different moves, from basic jabs to ridiculous wrestling maneuvers like the "Shoryuken" parody "Ball Buster." You can also buy stat upgrades — increase your health, attack power, or defense. The "Masochist" system means the more damage you take, the stronger your God Hand becomes. It's a clever way to keep you fighting even when you're losing. I've spent hours just tweaking my combo strings, trying to find the perfect flow.

The Enemies — Absurd, Difficult, and Hilarious

The enemy design in God Hand is something else. You'll fight mooks in leotards, demonic gorillas, a giant enemy crab (yes, really), and a pair of twin sisters who fight with their hair. The game throws groups of enemies at you that gang up relentlessly — they'll surround you, attack from behind, and juggle you across the arena if you're not careful. The difficulty is brutal, especially on your first playthrough. But unlike some hard games, God Hand doesn't feel unfair. Every enemy has tells, every boss has patterns, and every challenge has a solution. It's about learning, adapting, and eventually dominating. And when you do — when you perfect dodge a boss's attack and enter God Hand mode, then unleash a custom combo that sends them flying — it's one of the most satisfying feelings in gaming.

God Hand doesn't take itself seriously at all. The dialogue is absurd, the cutscenes are ridiculous, and the tone is pure camp. Your character Gene is a sarcastic jerk. Another where you battle a demon in a French mansion while dramatic music plays. The game never stops being funny, even when it's kicking your teeth in. Is God Hand Worth Playing in 2026?

Absolutely. God Hand is one of those games that has only grown in reputation over time. When it released, critics were divided — the difficulty turned some people off, and the humor was too weird for others. But now? It's recognized as a cult classic, one of Capcom's most unique and creative games. The combat system influenced titles like Bayonetta and Devil May Cry. The humor predates the "absurdist internet" aesthetic by years. And on PPSSPP, it plays perfectly. The 280MB ISO is a steal.
