The Roster — Who's Here?

The PSP version has a reduced roster compared to the console original, but the heavy hitters are here. Goku (Super Saiyan forms), Vegeta (Super Saiyan), and Frieza from Dragon Ball Z. Naruto (Naruto, Kurama Link mode), Sasuke (Eternal Mangekyo Sharingan), and Kakashi from Naruto. Luffy (Gear Fourth), Zoro, and Sabo from One Piece. Ichigo (Hollowfied) and Rukia from Bleach. Gon, Killua, and Hisoka from Hunter x Hunter. Yugi (with Exodia) from Yu-Gi-Oh!. Kenshiro from Fist of the North Star. It's a solid selection for a fan-made portable port. Each character has their signature moves — Kamehameha, Rasengan, Gum-Gum Pistol, Getsuga Tensho. The animations are flashy and satisfying.

Combat — Fast, Flashy, and Fun

The combat system is designed for accessibility. Light attack, heavy attack, jump, block, and special moves mapped to simple button combinations. You can dash, teleport behind opponents, and launch them into the air for combos. The arena damage is dynamic — characters get punched through buildings, slammed into the ground, and thrown off cliffs. The ultimate moves are cinematic — Goku's Super Kamehameha fills the screen, Naruto's Six Paths Rasenshuriken explodes, and Luffy's King Kong Gun crushes everything. The controls translate well to PPSSPP, though a controller is recommended for complex combos. The PSP version simplifies the system, but the core fun remains.

Story Mode — Cheesy But Fun

The story mode is original content. A new villain named "Galactic" is merging worlds and corrupting heroes. You create a custom character (who can learn moves from various series) and team up with Goku, Naruto, and Luffy to save reality. The cutscenes are cheesy — the dialogue is straight from a fan fiction. But seeing Goku and Luffy fight side-by-side is worth the price of admission. The PSP version includes the full story mode, though some cutscenes are compressed. The missions are standard — beat up enemies, rescue allies, fight bosses. It's repetitive, but the fan service makes it tolerable.

Step 3: If it's a ZIP file, extract it using ZArchiver or any file manager.
Step 4: Open PPSSPP, tap "Games", and navigate to the folder containing the ISO.
Step 5: Tap the game to launch it.
Step 6: Go to Settings > Graphics. Set "Rendering Resolution" to 2x or 3x PSP for sharper visuals. Enable "Buffered Rendering" and "Hardware Transform".
Step 7: For smoother performance, enable "Frame Skipping" at 1.
Step 8: A controller is highly recommended — the combat has a lot of inputs, and touchscreens can be cramped.

Pro Tips From Anime Fight Club:
• Learn the teleport mechanic (square + X). It's essential for escaping combos.
• Goku's Kamehameha is powerful, but slow. Use it as a punish, not a neutral tool.
• Naruto's Rasengan is great for closing distance. Dash forward and unleash it.

Custom Character — Make Your Own Anime Hero

One of the coolest features of Jump Force is the custom character system. You can create your own avatar, choosing their appearance, gender, voice, and fighting style. You can equip moves from any series — imagine a character who can use Kamehameha (Dragon Ball), Rasengan (Naruto), and Gum-Gum Pistol (One Piece). The customization is deep — skills, costumes, accessories, and even aura colors. The PSP version includes the custom character system, though the options are limited compared to the console original. Still, creating your own anime hero and fighting alongside Goku is a dream come true for fans.

The Warts — Honest Talk

Jump Force on PSP is a fan-made conversion, not an official release. The roster is smaller (20+ vs 40+). The graphics are downgraded significantly. The frame rate can drop during ultimate moves. The audio is compressed — some voice lines sound tinny. The story mode is repetitive. But given that the PSP hardware wasn't designed for modern 3D fighting games, it's a miracle this port exists at all. The core appeal — seeing Goku fight Naruto — is still there.
