Konami's Castlevania Anniversary Trailer Quietly Teased the Two 3D Games It's Never Re-Released

Published: September 27, 2026 by Oli Sa 6 min read
Simon Belmont walks past four framed portraits. Two of them are games Konami has kept locked on the PlayStation 2 for two decades.

Konami put out a video this week to mark 40 years of Castlevania, and buried in its final seconds is the most interesting thing the publisher has signalled about the series in years.

Near the end, Simon Belmont walks through a room in Dracula's castle lined with framed portraits. Each frame holds cover art from a Castlevania game. Four of them matter: Castlevania: Belmont's Curse, the new entry arriving next month; Castlevania: Lords of Shadow; and the two that have everyone talking, Castlevania: Lament of Innocence and Castlevania: Curse of Darkness.

Those last two have never been re-released. Not once.

Why those two are the story

It is worth being precise here, because a lot of the coverage has rounded this off to "a 3D collection" and lost the detail that makes it notable.

Three of the four portraits are 3D-era games. Lords of Shadow, the 2010 MercurySteam reboot, is already purchasable; it has been on Steam for years. Its presence in the video is unremarkable on its own.

Lament of Innocence (PlayStation 2, 2003) and Curse of Darkness (PlayStation 2, 2005) are a different matter entirely. Neither has ever appeared on a modern console. Neither is on PC. They have never been remastered, never been rolled into a Konami anniversary bundle, and never turned up on a subscription service. If you want to play either one legitimately in 2026, you need original PS2 hardware and a disc, or a PS3, in the narrow window where backward compatibility still applies.

For a publisher that has spent the last several years aggressively repackaging its back catalogue, that omission has always been conspicuous. The Castlevania Anniversary Collection covered the 8- and 16-bit era. The Advance Collection handled the Game Boy Advance games. The Dominus Collection brought the DS trilogy forward. The 3D games were left behind every single time.

So when Konami frames those two specific covers and puts them on screen during a 40th anniversary video, it is not nostalgia. It is inventory.

How Konami teases things

There is a pattern worth knowing. Konami has form for planting announcements inside celebratory videos months ahead of confirming them, and the community has learned to freeze-frame accordingly. Putting unavailable games in a shot alongside a game releasing in three weeks is not how you decorate a video by accident. Art departments do not pull cover scans for titles the company has no plans for.

The reasonable read: a collection of the 3D Castlevania games exists in some form, and it lands after Belmont's Curse has had its run. Konami is seeding it now so the idea is already circulating when it is ready to say something.

The unreasonable read, and it is out there, is that these are full remakes. Nothing in the video supports that. A compilation with modern resolution support, save states and a gallery is the shape this almost certainly takes.

What Konami has actually confirmed

Plenty, as it happens. The anniversary slate is genuinely substantial, and all of it is on the record:

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse launches October 15, 2026 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC via Steam, and Nintendo Switch.

The original Castlevania is free on the App Store and Google Play until October 24, 2026. No catch. It is a limited-time giveaway of the 1986 game.

A franchise sale of up to 80% off is running across storefronts: PlayStation Store from September 23 to October 7, and Steam from September 24 to October 1.

The Castlevania Black & Red soundtrack arrived on streaming platforms on September 26.

"Castlevania 40th Anniversary An Orchestral Concert" is booked for spring 2027 in London, Tokyo and Los Angeles, alongside a merchandise line.

That is a publisher treating a dormant-ish franchise like a priority again, which on its own is the better news story. The portrait scene is the part that hints at what comes after.

Twenty-three years on one console

Konami has not announced a 3D Castlevania collection. What it has done is put Lament of Innocence and Curse of Darkness on screen, framed and lit, in a video celebrating the series' anniversary. Those are two games it has pointedly refused to re-release through four separate compilations.

Twenty-three years is a long time to leave a game on one console. The portraits suggest Konami has finally decided it does not have to stay that way.
