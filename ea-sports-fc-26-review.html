EA SPORTS FC 26 Review: Best Football Game Yet or Just Another Update?

Published: April 29, 2026 by Oli Sa 10 min read
"After years of player frustration and the lowest sales in franchise history with FC 25, EA finally listened. The result is the best on-pitch gameplay in years—but a controversial new Season Pass threatens to undo the goodwill."

Let's be honest: EA Sports FC 25 was not well-received. Reports indicated it was the lowest-selling entry in the franchise's history, a wake-up call that forced EA to reconsider its strategy. With FC 26, EA came out with a clear message: "We finally listened to the players." And for the most part, they delivered. This year's release is the best-playing football game in years, with meaningful improvements across almost every mode. But in classic EA fashion, they also introduced one of the most aggressive monetization schemes we've seen—a Season Pass that locks iconic players like Ronaldinho and Cruyff behind a paywall, even in Career Mode. Let's break down everything you need to know.

Authentic vs. Competitive: A Genius Split

The headline feature of FC 26 is the introduction of two distinct gameplay presets: Competitive and Authentic. For years, players complained that online balancing ruined offline Career Mode and vice versa. This year, EA finally separated them.

Competitive mode is fast, aggressive, and geared toward online play. Passes are crisp, dribbling is responsive, and matches are high-scoring. Authentic mode is slower, more tactical, and emphasizes realistic physics—wind affects crosses, wet pitches slow the ball, and midfield battles actually matter. The downside? Authentic is locked to offline modes only. You can't use it in Squad Battles or any online modes, which feels like a missed opportunity.

Gameplay: The Best In Years

On the pitch, FC 26 is a revelation compared to the frustrating FC 25. Dribbling is sticky and responsive—you can actually feel the difference between a technical player and a physical one. Passing is crisper, with well-weighted through balls actually finding their target. Goalkeepers received a massive AI boost; they no longer parry shots directly into the path of attackers.

Defending, however, is a hot topic. AI defending has been significantly weakened—you can't rely on the computer to do the work for you anymore. This is great for skilled players who want to manually defend, but casuals will struggle. As one reviewer noted, "It has become extra difficult to defend." Matches in Competitive mode are often goal-fests, with scorelines like 6-4 or 8-7 becoming the norm.

Career Mode: A Long-Overdue Overhaul

Career Mode receives the biggest upgrade in years. The new Manager Live feature offers dynamic challenges—take a club with a 20-point deduction to safety, or lead Real Madrid to a domestic double while starting Bellingham in 25 matches. The new Unexpected Events system throws curveballs at you: players injuring each other in training, food poisoning before big games, or club takeovers altering your budget.

The coaching market lets you move clubs based on your achievements and playstyle. If you're a possession-based manager, teams that prioritize possession will come calling. For the first time, Icons and Heroes like Ronaldinho and Zola are available in Career Mode—but this is where EA's monetization rears its ugly head. Many of these Icons are locked behind the Season Pass. You literally cannot play as them unless you grind (or pay).

Ultimate Team: Monetization Overload

Ultimate Team feels smoother with the return of tournaments and Evolution cosmetics, but the power curve has been intentionally slowed. In theory, this makes your cards viable longer. In practice, rewards have been nerfed across the board, making it even harder for free-to-play players to compete.

The new cross-mode Season Pass is the real controversy. For the first time, Ultimate Team's monetization has bled into Career Mode and Clubs. Want to use Miroslav Klose or Park Ji-Sung in your offline Career Mode? Grind Ultimate Team or spend money. It's a decision that has rightfully angered the community. "This might be the worst version of a Season Pass in a sports game yet," wrote IGN in their review.

The Good

  • Authentic & Competitive modes cater to all playstyles
  • Dribbling and passing are the smoothest in years
  • Goalkeepers finally act like professionals
  • Manager Live breathes life into Career Mode
  • Unexpected Events add realism and unpredictability
  • Post-launch patches show EA is listening

The Bad

  • Aggressive Season Pass locks Icons behind paywalls
  • Defending is incredibly difficult for casual players
  • Authentic mode locked to offline only
  • Ultimate Team rewards nerfed, still pay-to-win
  • Career Mode story depth still lacking
  • $70 price tag feels steep for an iterative update

Patch Updates: A New Hope

To EA's credit, post-launch support has been strong. Title Update v1.4.0 (January 2026) weakened AI defenders who previously stuck to attackers like glue. Squad Battles AI was reworked to play more aggressively, and acceleration types were refined so more players qualify for the "Lengthy" run style. Update v1.5.0 (March 2026) further balanced PlayStyles, improved acrobatic shot accuracy, and added new Career Mode content including Ronaldinho and Gareth Bale. These patches demonstrate EA's commitment to listening—a welcome change after years of neglect.

Final Verdict: Step Forward, But...

EA Sports FC 26 is the best-playing football game in years. The split between Authentic and Competitive modes is a masterstroke that finally respects different player preferences. Career Mode feels alive again with Manager Live and Unexpected Events. The on-pitch gameplay—dribbling, passing, goalkeeping—is fluid and satisfying.

However, EA's worsening monetization cannot be ignored. The Season Pass locking Icons behind paywalls, even in offline Career Mode, is a cynical cash grab. Ultimate Team remains aggressively pay-to-win despite the slower power curve.

Who should buy it? If you're a Career Mode enthusiast who can ignore the locked Icons, or a competitive online player, this is a significant upgrade. If you're a casual player happy with FC 25 or FC 24, skip it—the core experience isn't different enough to justify $70. EA has taken a step forward, but the monetization keeps it from being a true champion.

Final Rating: 8/10
"FC 26 is EA at its best—and its worst. The gameplay is a triumph, but the greed is back with a vengeance."
